## Design Specificity Verdict

**LLM assessment**: Grounded, with one serious crack. Real CGS-liveried trucks and project cargo throughout, domain-fluent copy ("packing list analysis," "self-geared and semi-geared vessels," "lashing and lifting calculations"), a real address/UEN/ISO 9001:2015 credential repeated three times, and a sector list that reads as pulled from an actual client document. The layout-family discipline (bento → editorial rows → split → horizontal gallery → chip list) delivers genuine visual variety instead of five stacked card rows. The crack: the video band directly below the hero — the first full-bleed motion content a visitor sees — carries a visible "KlingAI 3.0" watermark in the bottom-right corner, on a page whose entire credibility pitch rests on "real operations, verifiable."

**Deterministic scan**: The static-source CLI scan (`detect.mjs` against `index.php`) came back clean — `[]`, exit 0, zero findings. The live-DOM browser scan (running the same detector against the fully rendered page) found 23-24 anti-pattern matches: 13× `ai-color-palette` ("cyan neon text on dark background"), 4× `dark-glow` (colored box-shadow on dark background), 2× `low-contrast` (3.7:1, needs 4.5:1, text `#7a869a` on `#ffffff`), 1× `gpt-thin-border-wide-shadow` (0.8px border + 48px shadow blur), 1× `clipped-overflow-container` (`.cgs-hero__swiper` clips a positioned child), and 1× `overused-font` (Plus Jakarta Sans, 100% of text) and 1x `monotonous-spacing` (~16px used 93% of the time).

Of these, roughly 17 of 23 (the `ai-color-palette` and `dark-glow` findings) are near-certain **false positives**: the detector is pattern-matching a generic "AI slop neon-on-dark" signature against this project's own documented, deliberate brand tokens — `--cgs-cyan-400` (#2E9FD6) and `--cgs-navy-900` (#082238), both explicitly sampled and validated against Maersk's live palette per `CLAUDE.md` and `DESIGN.md`. `overused-font` is also a false positive by design: `CLAUDE.md` specifies one typeface site-wide on purpose. `gpt-thin-border-wide-shadow` likely collides with the same intentional-token pattern (the hairline border + `--cgs-shadow-lg`'s 48px blur documented in DESIGN.md's Tinted-Shadow Rule), though with less certainty than the color findings. The remaining two — **`low-contrast` and `clipped-overflow-container`** — are not covered by any documented brand rule and read as genuine.

The design-review LLM independently verified that `DESIGN.md`'s own rules actually hold on the live page: no gradient scrim anywhere (No-Scrim Rule), cyan-400 fills a surface in exactly one place (Single-Fill Rule), nothing carries a shadow at rest (Flat-at-Rest Rule). Notably, the one real credibility problem found on this page — an AI-generated video with a visible tool watermark — is something the mechanical scan cannot see at all (it inspects CSS/DOM patterns, not video content); only the LLM review caught it.

**Visual overlays**: Assessment B injected the detector into a live tab and confirmed overlay boxes rendered directly on the page (labeled "overused font," "monotonous spacing," "glowing shadow accents," "hairline border with wide shadow," "low contrast text," clustered around the header/hero stat-bar). Per the critique workflow, the live-server used to serve the overlay script was stopped once evidence-gathering finished, so no overlay is live in your browser right now — the findings above are the full record of what it showed.

## Overall Impression

The design system itself is unusually well-executed for how early this project is — `DESIGN.md`'s own rules (No-Scrim, Single-Fill, Flat-at-Rest, the 4px shape discipline) were written to describe what's already true of the shipped CSS, not aspirational goals, and both assessments independently confirm that. The gap isn't craft, it's honesty of content: an AI-watermarked video presented as real footage, and unmarked placeholder copy making ownership claims a buyer would verify, both sit in direct tension with a page whose entire pitch is "real, ISO-certified, verifiable." The single biggest opportunity is closing that gap before this goes in front of an actual industrial buyer — the visual system doesn't need more polish nearly as much as the content needs to be true to what it claims.

3. **Real, specific operations photography** (branded trucks, actual project cargo, real dockside shots) does trust-building work generic logistics stock photography never could.

## Priority Issues

**[P0] AI-generated video with a visible "KlingAI 3.0" watermark in the home page's second section**
- **Why it matters**: This is the second thing a visitor sees, positioned by the brief as real operations footage, on a page whose entire value proposition rests on "real, ISO 9001:2015 certified, verifiable." A buyer who spots the watermark will reasonably wonder what else on the page is fabricated — and it lands before any of the genuinely strong real photography gets a chance to counteract it.
- **Fix**: Replace with real footage, or if none exists yet, mark the video band as a placeholder/concept asset the same way copy placeholders are supposed to be marked — not presented as documentary footage.
- **Suggested command**: `/impeccable harden`

**[P1] Unmarked placeholder copy making unverified operational claims (fleet split section)**
- **Why it matters**: `index.php`'s own top comment states hero, approach, fleet, and CTA copy are placeholder that the client has supplied for none of it — yet the fleet-split section states as fact that CGS owns its trailers, runs its own lashing crew, and operates its own yard, with zero visible marking. This is exactly the "claims a buyer will verify" scenario `CLAUDE.md` warns against inventing. (Note: the file also carries a second, contradictory comment claiming the approach-section copy is client-verbatim — worth resolving which is true in the source itself.)
- **Fix**: Get client sign-off and remove from the placeholder list, or visibly mark as draft until confirmed, per `CLAUDE.md`'s own rule.
- **Suggested command**: `/impeccable clarify`

**[P1] Secondary text fails WCAG AA contrast in at least two places**
- **Why it matters**: The detector's live-DOM scan measured `#7a869a` (Muted Slate) on white at 3.7:1, below the 4.5:1 minimum for body text. This isn't covered by any documented brand exception — it's a genuine accessibility gap that affects any low-vision or Sam-persona (screen-reader/keyboard, low-contrast-sensitive) visitor reading secondary copy.
- **Fix**: Darken Muted Slate's use for body-weight text, or reserve it strictly for large/decorative text where the 3:1 threshold applies instead.
- **Suggested command**: `/impeccable audit`

**[P1] Hero carousel has zero visible pagination, arrows, or pause control, inconsistent with the services section a few scrolls later**
- **Why it matters**: Two nearly identical "step through several branded messages" patterns behave completely differently on the same page. A visitor has no way to know a second or third hero message exists and no way to manually reach it, while the services section — doing the same job — gives full visible control.
- **Fix**: At minimum, add a slide-position indicator (dots or "n/N") to the hero, matching the visibility standard the page already sets for itself in the services section.
- **Suggested command**: `/impeccable polish`

**[P2] Non-Home nav destinations silently render the homepage instead of a distinct page or a proper 404**
- **Why it matters**: `/our-fleet.php`, `/services.php`, `/contact.php`, `/projects.php` and every service-dropdown link don't exist as files yet (confirmed: only `404.php`, `index.php`, `landing.php` exist at the project root) and the dev server falls back to serving `index.php`'s content with only the nav highlight changed — worse than a 404, since it hides that anything went wrong.
- **Fix**: Confirm production 404 behavior actually differs from this dev fallback before launch; build the missing pages or gate the nav links until they exist.
- **Suggested command**: `/impeccable harden`

**[P2] `resources.php` has no entry point anywhere in the home page's nav or footer**
- **Why it matters**: It's one of the ten pages `CLAUDE.md` documents for this site, but a visitor arriving on the home page has no way to discover it exists.
- **Fix**: Add it to the primary nav or footer link list.
- **Suggested command**: `/impeccable layout`

**[P3] Detector flagged `.cgs-hero__swiper` as clipping a positioned child element**
- **Why it matters**: A real, non-brand-related structural finding — worth a quick look even though visual QA on desktop and mobile found no obvious breakage. May or may not be the same clipping Assessment A separately observed on mobile (the hero facts card pushed to the viewport's bottom edge on a 390×844 screen).
- **Fix**: Inspect `.cgs-hero__swiper`'s overflow behavior against its absolutely-positioned children (the facts card, the eyebrow pill) at narrow viewports.
- **Suggested command**: `/impeccable adapt`
